Turning Around the National Incubation Centres, Lahore and Quetta

The Vice Chancellor of LUMS asked me to take over the National Incubation Centres (NIC) in Lahore and Quetta and put them back on track. I did this alongside my main job as Head of Research Operations, in the middle of COVID-19.

Organisation: LUMS, PakistanRole: Senior Project ManagerFunder: Ignite (National Technology Fund)Period: June 2020 to August 2021

The Situation

When I took over, the centres were in a difficult place:

  • Technical and financial deliverables owed to the funder were behind schedule.
  • Ignite had put its payments on hold until those deliverables were met.
  • COVID-19 had stopped all in-person mentoring, which is the heart of an incubator.

The previous management had been removed. Startups were still in the programme and needed support, and the team needed clear direction.

What I Did

1. Got the right people in the right roles

I restructured both centres. I brought in new staff, moved existing team members into roles that suited their skills, and set clear responsibilities using a RACI chart. Everyone knew what they owned and who they reported to.

2. Cleared the backlog with the funder

I worked through the pending technical and financial deliverables one by one. Where COVID-19 had made the original plan impossible, I submitted formal change requests to Ignite. Once the backlog was cleared and the changes approved, the held funding was released.

3. Moved mentoring online

Startups could not come to the centre, so we took the centre to them. I set up online mentoring with LUMS faculty and external industry experts, and we graduated the existing cohort digitally.

4. Kept new startups coming in

We revamped the website and ran a fully virtual recruitment campaign. Over the pandemic period the centres enrolled 2 new cohorts and graduated 3.

5. Built a model that lasted

The online learning model we set up did not end with the lockdown. It later became the centres' hybrid model, mixing online sessions with in-person work at the centre.

Hosting Senior Stakeholders

Part of the job was showing funders and partners that the centres were working again. During this period we hosted the British High Commissioner, the CEO of Ignite and other senior stakeholders. Founders presented their work directly to the visitors, which gave them exposure they would not normally get.

The Result

By August 2021 both centres were meeting their deliverables, funding was flowing again, and the incubation programme was running on its new hybrid model. I handed over to new management with both centres fully on track and went back to my full-time role in Research Operations.
